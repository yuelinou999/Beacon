**Response:**

**Concept:** This problem requires solving a **two-step linear equation**. To solve for the variable $x$, we must use the properties of equality to isolate $x$ on one side of the equation. This means performing the same operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division) to both sides of the equals sign to keep the equation balanced.

Here is the step-by-step solution:

Step 1: Isolate the term containing $x$ by eliminating the constant added to it.
$$3x + 7 = 22$$
We use the **Subtraction Property of Equality**, which states that if we subtract a number from both sides of an equation, the equation remains true. We subtract 7 from both sides:
$$3x + 7 - 7 = 22 - 7$$
$$3x = 15$$

Step 2: Solve for $x$ by eliminating the coefficient 3.
The term $3x$ means 3 multiplied by $x$. To undo this multiplication, we use the **Division Property of Equality**, dividing both sides by 3:
$$\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{15}{3}$$
$$x = 5$$

Step 3: Verification
To verify the solution, substitute $x=5$ back into the original equation:
$$3(5) + 7 = 22$$
$$15 + 7 = 22$$
$$22 = 22$$
Since the left side equals the right side, the solution is correct.

**Answer: x = 5**

Since we are looking for the original number, we will use an equation to represent this relationship and then solve for the unknown variable.

**Step 1: Write the equation.**
Let $x$ represent the unknown number. The phrase "$\frac{2}{3}$ of a number is 18" translates directly into the equation:
$$\frac{2}{3}x = 18$$

**Step 2: Isolate the variable $x$.**
To solve for $x$, we must undo the multiplication by $\frac{2}{3}$. We do this by multiplying both sides of the equation by the **reciprocal** of $\frac{2}{3}$, which is $\frac{3}{2}$.

**Step 3: Calculate the value of $x$.**

$$x = \left(\frac{18}{2}\right) \cdot 3$$

**Step 4: Verify the answer.**
To check if 27 is correct, we calculate $\frac{2}{3}$ of 27:
$$\frac{2}{3} \cdot 27 = \frac{2 \cdot 27}{3} = \frac{54}{3} = 18$$
Since the result (18) matches the given information, the number is correct.

**Response:**

**Concept and Formula Explanation:**
The concept used to solve this problem is **Newton's Second Law of Motion**, which relates the net force acting on an object to its mass and acceleration. This law states that the net force ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}}$) acting on an object is equal to the product of its mass ($m$) and its acceleration ($a$).

The formula is:
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a$$
This formula applies because we are given the mass and the resulting acceleration, and we need to find the single net force causing this change in motion.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

Step 1: Identify the given values.
We are given:
*   Mass ($m$) = $5 \, \text{kg}$
*   Acceleration ($a$) = $3 \, \text{m/s}^2$
*   Net Force ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}}$) = ?

Step 2: Write down the relevant formula.
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a$$

Step 3: Substitute the known values into the formula.
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = (5 \, \text{kg}) \cdot (3 \, \text{m/s}^2)$$

Step 4: Calculate the result by multiplying the mass and the acceleration.
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = 15 \, \text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2$$

Step 5: State the final unit for force.
In physics, the standard unit for force derived from these base units ($\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2$) is the **Newton ($\text{N}$)**.
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = 15 \, \text{N}$$

**Verification:**
We substitute the values back into the original equation:
$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = (5 \, \text{kg}) \times (3 \, \text{m/s}^2) = 15 \, \text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2$$
Since $1 \, \text{N} = 1 \, \text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2$, the unit consistency confirms that the calculation is correct, resulting in a force of $15 \text{ N}$.

**Answer: 15 N**
